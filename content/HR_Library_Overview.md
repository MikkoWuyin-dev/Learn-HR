# Your HR Library — A Friendly Overview

Six files, one plan. Here's what you're holding and how to live with it.

## The short version

You're starting from zero in HR, in Nigeria, with no money to spend on courses. The documents below take you from there to being ready to apply for a first job as a recruiter or HR assistant — 24 weeks at about seven hours a week, everything free, nothing requiring you to beg for access.

If you read only one thing today, make it Document 1; it takes ten minutes. Then open Document 2 and do the Week 0 row. Everything else can wait until it's needed.

## The five documents

**Document 1 — Start Here.** A one-sitting read. It gives you the twenty-five words you'll meet in every course, then shows how to study so the knowledge sticks. There's a short setup for your four free accounts and your work folder, and it finishes by describing what a person in your first job is expected to be able to do. Read it once, then keep the glossary page beside you while you study.

**Document 2 — The 24-Week Plan.** This is the one you'll open most. Each week is a table of short blocks — forty-five to seventy-five minutes each — and every block ends with something you produce: a summary, a template, a script, a spreadsheet. Nothing in it needs a payment card or an application form, and if a week's output exists the week counts as passed even if you didn't finish the videos. At the back you'll find a tracker of nineteen courses and sixteen pieces of work, along with a page on what to do when you fall behind (you will, twice, and that's fine).

**Document 3 — The Resource Library.** One place where every course lives: the link, what it really costs, how long it takes, and what to make of it. The rule for being in here is simple: the platform has to offer HR courses, HR qualifications, or HR job listings, and the free part has to be free all the way through. That's why you'll find Saylor, the Open University, CIPD, Alison, the ILO training centre, AIHR and the two big ATS academies here — and why plenty of well-known names are missing. A short list at the back records what was left out and the reason for each, so you don't have to go and rediscover it.

**Document 4 — Nigeria: Labour Law & CIPM.** Weeks ten to twelve, plus something to keep. It covers what the Labour Act actually says about written terms, notice periods, hours, sick leave, annual leave, redundancy and maternity — read from the government's own copy of the law, with each section number given, so you can check it yourself. It then covers the professional body, CIPM: what it costs and when it starts to be worth your money. You'll also find the twenty tasks a junior HR person is actually handed, a page of common sayings about Nigerian law that turn out to be wrong, and a one-page card worth copying out by hand.

**Document 5 — Getting the Job.** Weeks twenty-two to twenty-four, and the bits you'll come back to for years. It sorts out which of the three different "HR" jobs you're actually after, gives you a short honest story about why you're changing direction, walks you through a CV that survives the software employers use to scan them, sets up your LinkedIn, and lays out a small system for sending applications and following up. The interview section holds the fifteen questions you're most likely to face, with a simple shape for answering them. The last pages cover the exercise you may be given and how to hold a salary conversation. There are warning signs for job scams, and messages you can copy when someone goes quiet.

## How they fit together

Live in Document 2. When a week names a course, jump to Document 3 for the link. Document 4 is your companion for weeks ten to twelve and your reference whenever a law question comes up. Document 5 waits until week twenty-two. Document 1 explains how the whole thing is meant to work.

## A few honest notes

The plan assumes about seven hours a week. Five hours a week gets you there in roughly thirty-two weeks, and the order of priorities doesn't change.

Everything was checked on 20 October 2026, and free offers on the internet do shift. Spend a minute on the enrolment page before you build a week around a course.

The figures that move — the minimum wage, CIPM's fees, pension percentages — are written with the date you should trust them until, and with a nudge to confirm before you quote them to anyone.

Alongside these five you'll find one larger file, `HR_Roadmap_Library`, which holds the full research this was trimmed from, including the non-HR learning options. Keep it for later; work from the five.

## Start tonight, in one hour

Read Document 1's word list twice and write your own version of it. Create accounts at Saylor, OpenLearn, Alison and CIPD. Make a folder called `HR Portfolio` and a sheet called `Recruiting Tracker`. Put five weekly time slots in your calendar.

That's Week 0 done, and you'll already be ahead of most people who say they're going to learn HR this year.
