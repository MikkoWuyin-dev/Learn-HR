## 1.1 THE 15-WORD MAP OF HR

**HR gets business results through people — hiring them, developing them, paying them, keeping them, and letting them go well.** Everything you will study for six months is one of those five verbs.

Your target job is the first verb: **Recruiter / Talent Acquisition.**

---

## 1.2 THE 25 WORDS YOU NEED ON DAY ONE

Learn these and every course becomes a third easier. Copy the right-hand column into your own words.

| Term | Plain-English meaning |
|---|---|
| **Talent Acquisition (TA)** | Planned, data-driven hiring. The strategic version of "recruitment." |
| **Recruiter / TA Associate** | The person who runs hiring for open roles — your first job. |
| **Sourcing** | Hunting candidates directly instead of waiting for CVs to arrive. |
| **Applicant Tracking System (ATS)** | The software holding jobs, applicants, pipeline stages and notes. |
| **Job description (JD)** | The public advert. |
| **Job analysis** | The research behind the JD: what the job actually requires. |
| **Requisition ("req")** | The formally approved request to hire. No req, no hiring. |
| **Pipeline / funnel** | Candidates at each stage: applied → screened → interviewed → offered → hired. |
| **Screening call** | 15–30 minutes to filter before a real interview. |
| **Structured interview** | Same questions, same scoring scale, every candidate. The professional standard. |
| **Scorecard** | The form interviewers fill in to rate a candidate against agreed criteria. |
| **Hiring manager** | The person the hire reports to. Your internal client. |
| **Time-to-fill** | Days from req opening to offer accepted. The metric you'll be judged on. |
| **Cost-per-hire** | Total hiring cost ÷ number of hires. |
| **Offer acceptance rate** | Offers accepted ÷ offers made. |
| **Source of hire** | Which channel produced the people you actually hired. Tells you where to spend effort. |
| **Quality of hire** | How new hires perform and stay past 6–12 months. |
| **Headcount / workforce planning** | Deciding how many people, with which skills, before hiring starts. |
| **Onboarding** | The first 30/60/90 days. Bad onboarding is the top cause of early quitting. |
| **Employee lifecycle** | Attract → hire → onboard → develop → reward → retain → separate. The spine of HR. |
| **HR generalist** | Covers the whole lifecycle for a team or site. Where recruiters usually grow into. |
| **HR Business Partner (HRBP)** | Senior advisor to a business unit. Not an entry-level job. |
| **HRIS** | The system of record for employee data (the ATS is for applicants). |
| **Total rewards** | Everything of value an employee gets: pay, benefits, well-being, career, recognition. |
| **Employee relations (ER)** | Conflict, discipline, grievances, investigations. |
| **Redundancy** | The role goes, not the person. In Nigeria the Labour Act's "last in, first out" rule applies to covered workers. |

## 1.3 THE METHOD (more important than motivation)

- **Produce, don't just watch.** 60 minutes of study earns 20 minutes of writing. No output = no learning.
- **Recall before you review.** Open every session by writing, from memory, what you learned last time. Five minutes. It roughly halves forgetting.
- **Space it out.** 45 minutes × 5 days beats 4 hours on Sunday. Doc 2 is built in small blocks for this reason.
- **Keep one "artefacts" folder from day one.** Everything you write goes there; it becomes your portfolio in week 21 without extra effort.

## 1.4 YOUR ₦0 TOOLKIT (45 minutes, once)

1. **A dedicated email address** for HR sign-ups and applications.
2. **Google Docs or Word Online** (free in a browser) — one document called `HR Learning Log`.
3. **Google Sheets or Excel Online** — one file called `Recruiting Tracker`. Columns: Date · Name · Role · Source · Stage · Notes · Next action.
4. **A Drive/OneDrive folder `HR Portfolio`** — every artefact lands here.
5. **A free LinkedIn account.** Don't optimise it yet; you'll have something worth showing in Doc 5.
6. **Calendar blocks.** Book five slots for the next four weeks now: e.g. Tue 8–9pm, Wed 8–9pm, Fri 7–8pm, Sat 9am–12pm, Sun 30-min review. If it isn't in the calendar, it won't happen.

That's the whole setup. No paid software, no plugin, no course bundle.

## 1.5 HOW TO SPOT A FAKE "FREE"

Three tests, applicable to any resource, ever:

1. **Does it ask for card details before showing the content?** Then it's a trial.
2. **Is the payment for the learning or just the certificate?** If learning is free and the PDF costs money, you're fine — take the knowledge, skip the paper.
3. **Is the credential verifiable?** A certificate with no public verification link is decoration. Prefer artefacts and badges you can show over JPGs of diplomas.

## 1.6 WHAT "JOB-READY" MEANS (your target)

By the end of the plan you should be able to say, out loud, with examples:

- Turn a manager's brief into a **job analysis, job description and interview scorecard**.
- **Source** candidates on LinkedIn and Google, and explain your search strategy rather than just your search box.
- Run a **15-minute screen** and write a summary a hiring manager can act on.
- Explain **structured interviewing** and how you'd cut bias in a panel.
- Keep an **ATS** clean: stages, notes, tags, reasons for rejection.
- Build a **recruitment dashboard** in a spreadsheet: funnel conversion, time-to-fill, source of hire — and say what you'd change.
- State the **Nigerian basics** a recruiter gets asked: written particulars, notice periods, redundancy, sick and maternity leave, working hours, minimum wage, pensions (Doc 4).
- Describe how you use **AI** in hiring and where you refuse to.
- Say what **good candidate experience** looks like — including how you reject people.

That is the bar. If a week in Doc 2 doesn't move you toward one of those lines, it's padding — skip it.
