# Document 3 — The Resource Library

### Free HR courses and professional resources, deduplicated · every one free to finish without an application

*20 October 2026 · 11 resources in the core spine, 3 supporting bodies, 3 statutory sources, 3 job sources*

---

## THE RULE THIS LIBRARY FOLLOWS

An entry appears here only if the platform offers **HR or talent-acquisition courses, HR credentials, or HR/recruiting job listings**, **and** you can complete what's free without an application, an essay, a waiting period or card details.

That means the following are deliberately absent, and you shouldn't go looking for them here:

- **Anything gated behind financial aid.** Coursera removed free auditing in August 2025; the full-course route is now a per-course written application with a ~16-day wait. Not worth your stress when Saylor and OpenLearn give you the same ground immediately.
- **General-skills platforms with no HR content** (IBM SkillsBuild, Microsoft Learn, Google Skillshop, OpenAI Academy, Anthropic, HubSpot, Trailhead, Cursa, Kaggle), **open textbooks** (OpenStax), **generic Excel courses**, and **HR news/podcast feeds**. Useful in a broad sense; none of them will teach you recruitment or get you an HR job. If you later want them, the wider research version of this library is kept in `HR_Roadmap_Library.html`.
- **Paid-only or trial-only provider academies** (TA Institute, Skill India, Great Learning's certificate, NextInHR, Workday/SAP/Oracle/ADP — no public free certification exists for any of them) **and AIHR, whose free tier ended: its whole catalogue now sits behind the paid Academy**.

**AI was the one exception, and it no longer has a free course behind it.** AIHR's Generative AI course was the only free, HR-specific AI course that cleared this bar; AIHR has since moved its whole catalogue behind its paid Academy. You still cover the skill, through the prompt pack and the guardrails page you write in Week 19 — better evidence than a certificate anyway. Everything else about "learning AI" is optional for your first role.

## 3.1 THE SPINE — do these, in this order

#### Saylor Academy — PRDV226 *Introduction to Human Resource Management*
- **Link:** https://learn.saylor.org/course/PRDV226
- **Cost:** free to enrol, free to complete, **free certificate of completion** (no application, no fee; pass their exam at ≥70%)
- **Time:** ~18 hours · self-paced · **Level:** beginner
- **What it is:** a proper university-style introductory HRM course — reading and assignments, not hype video — covering HR's role, job analysis, recruitment, selection, performance, compensation and labour relations.
- **Why it matters:** the gentlest serious starting point that still ends in a free, real certificate.
- **Do this with it:** Weeks 1–2. One page of notes per unit; those pages become your interview crib. Failed a unit challenge? Re-read and retake (their rule: a second attempt after a 7-day wait).

#### Saylor Academy — BUS301 *Human Resource Management*
- **Link:** https://learn.saylor.org/course/BUS301
- **Cost:** free, including the final exam and certificate (≥70%)
- **Time:** ~24 hours · **Level:** beginner → intermediate
- **What it is:** the full HRM curriculum in seven units: HR strategy and analytics, job analysis, recruitment and selection, development, performance, compensation and benefits, labour relations and safety.
- **Why it matters:** the closest thing in the free world to a real HR module, and one of the very few places the certificate itself is free. It single-handedly gives you the professional vocabulary.
- **Do this with it:** the backbone of Phase 2 — one unit per week, aligned to that week's domain. Skip PRDV226 if BUS301 feels manageable; don't do both cover-to-cover.

#### The Open University — OpenLearn *Human resources: recruitment and selection*
- **Link:** https://www.open.edu/openlearn/ (search that exact title; content pages are readable with no account)
- **Cost:** free to study; **free Statement of Participation** when you create a free OpenLearn account and complete it
- **Time:** ~12 hours · **Level:** beginner
- **What it is:** a university course on the recruitment cycle — planning, advertising, shortlisting, selection methods, fairness, legal context. UK-framed, process-agnostic.
- **Why it matters:** the flagship free HR-specific course anywhere; written for people with zero background. A university-issued statement of participation carries real weight on a Nigerian, UK or Gulf CV.
- **Do this with it:** Week 4. Reuse its shortlisting logic when you build your own scorecard.

#### OpenLearn — three more HR courses (same provider, same terms)
| Course | Time | Use it in |
|---|---|---|
| **Developing your skills as an HR professional** | ~9 h | Week 2 — the "how to behave as a professional" course nobody teaches beginners |
| **Employee engagement** | ~10 h | Week 8 — engagement, voice, motivation |
| **Workplace learning with coaching and mentoring** | — | Week 5 — L&D in practice |
| **Human resource management and equality, diversity and inclusion** | — | Week 11 — fair, structured selection |
- **Link:** https://www.open.edu/openlearn/ · **Cost:** free to study, free Statement of Participation on completion
- **Do this with it:** claim every Statement. These are the only free credentials that say *a university saw you finish this*.

#### CIPD — the three free MOOCs
- **Link:** https://www.cipd.org/en/learning/mooc/ (each course links out to FutureLearn)
- **Courses:** *HR Fundamentals* (5 weeks) · *People Management Skills* (5 weeks) · *Inclusive Management and Leadership Skills* (4 weeks)
- **Cost:** free with **limited access** — content while the course is open to you; no graded certificate, no lifetime access. This is study, not credential.
- **Time:** ~3 hrs/week for 4–5 weeks
- **Why it matters:** the Chartered Institute of Personnel and Development is the UK's professional body for HR and the profession's de facto standard-setter. You get the profession's *own framing* of the job — the language multinational HR teams, NGOs and banks use, including in Lagos.
- **Do this with it:** enrol in HR Fundamentals in Week 1 so the questions you can't answer shape your syllabus. Note the access window when you join and finish inside it. Do **not** buy a FutureLearn upgrade to "keep access."

#### CIPD — the free factsheet hub
- **Link:** https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/factsheets/
- **Cost:** free (some items ask for a free CIPD account; a subset is members-only)
- **What it is:** ~100 regularly updated factsheets — recruitment and selection, reward, ER, L&D, employment law, HR technology.
- **Why it matters:** the reference shelf I'd hand any new HR officer. Concise, cited, updated. Better interview preparation than any YouTube channel.
- **Do this with it:** one factsheet per Phase 2 week, before you study that domain. Keep the 12 you use most in one folder and quote them in your artefacts.

#### ITCILO — free self-guided courses from the UN's training arm
- **Links:** https://www.itcilo.org/courses · https://www.itcilo.org/training
- **Cost:** many self-guided online courses are **free** (tutor-led e-learning is paid — filter for self-guided). A digital credential is issued once you complete the required activities (their stated rule of thumb: ≥80% of at least an hour of activity).
- **Time:** typically 4–8 hours
- **What it is:** the International Training Centre of the International Labour Organization — labour administration, workers' rights, employment policy, social dialogue.
- **Why it matters:** a UN-agency credential on an entry-level CV is a genuine differentiator, and it's the only free source here that gives you the *labour-market* framing private HR courses skip.
- **Do this with it:** **Introduction to Labour Administration** (Week 10) and **Business and Decent Work** (Week 11). Register on their eLearning platform; if a course shows as closed for now, note the next run rather than forcing it.

#### Workable Academy + their ATS guide
- **Links:** https://academy.workable.com/ · https://resources.workable.com/tutorial/applicant-tracking-systems-atoz
- **Cost:** free — their own FAQ says courses carry no additional fee; you do need to create an account (normally a Workable workspace)
- **What it is:** real recruiter-side product training — job setup, pipeline, screening, scheduling, reporting — plus a long, plain-language A-to-Z on what an ATS is and how to run one.
- **Why it matters:** "ATS experience" is the most-requested skill you don't have. Vendor training is the free way to close that gap, and their written guides are unusually good on process design.
- **Do this with it:** Week 18. Follow along in a free trial workspace so your notes become a portfolio walkthrough. Screenshot the stages, never candidate data.

#### Greenhouse Learn
- **Link:** https://learn.greenhouse.io/ · all courses: https://learn.greenhouse.io/page/all-courses
- **Cost:** free on-demand training and recorded sessions; no paid licence needed to learn the product
- **What it is:** training on one of the world's most-used ATS platforms — candidate profiles, stages, scorecards, disqualifying reasons, standard reports, report builder.
- **Why it matters:** Greenhouse is the standard ATS at most global and remote tech employers, and remote TA adverts name it. Knowing a modern ATS's vocabulary — stages, scorecards, dispositions, interview kits — makes you sound like an insider.
- **Do this with it:** Week 18, instead of or alongside Workable. Then write a one-page note: *how I'd configure a pipeline for a Nigerian SME.* That's an artefact and an interview answer at once.

## 3.2 PROFESSIONAL BODIES AND NIGERIAN INSTITUTIONS

#### CIPM Nigeria — the body you'll eventually pay
- **Links:** https://www.cipmnigeria.org/ · membership routes: https://www.cipmnigeria.org/membership/membership-routes/ · learning: https://www.cipmnigeria.org/learning/
- **Cost:** membership and exams are paid. On the examination route, roughly ₦10,000 application and ₦12,000 on acceptance, then per-paper/professional exam fees in the ₦20k–₦70k range (their own fee pages are the only reliable source; ignore blog totals). **You do not need this to start working in HR** — you need it to progress, usually 1–2 years in, ideally employer-funded.
- **Why it matters:** CIPM is the statutory professional body for HR practice in Nigeria, and its name appears in job adverts as "required" or "an advantage" more than any other credential here.
- **Do this with it:** read the membership-routes page in Week 1 so your plan ends on the right next step; attend their free events and webinars; use their published materials as your syllabus check for what a Nigerian HR professional is expected to know.

#### CIPM Nigeria — free *Nigerian Labour Law* study pack (PDF)
- **Link:** https://www.cipmnigeria.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/NIGERIAN-LABOUR-LAW.pdf
- **Cost:** **free**, published by the institute itself (4th edition)
- **What it is:** a professional-body textbook on Nigerian labour law — the Labour Act and its provisions (hours, sick leave, maternity, redundancy), trade unions, factories and related institutions, structured for exams.
- **Why it matters:** the single most valuable free document in this whole programme. Almost nothing else online explains Nigerian employment law at professional-exam depth for ₦0.
- **Do this with it:** Weeks 10–11, Part 1 only, slowly. Cross-check every figure against the statute text below. Then write your own 2-page "Nigerian employment law for recruiters" — that document is worth attaching to applications.

#### SHRM — free toolkits and templates (US-framed)
- **Links:** https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/tools/toolkits · https://www.shrm.org/credentials
- **Cost:** toolkits and templates are free downloads; articles are metered for non-members (~2/month); SHRM-CP itself is paid (~US$240–340 exam plus study materials) — a later, funded step, not now
- **Why it matters:** US-framed employers, multinationals and remote US-facing TA teams hire against SHRM language. Their free interview-question banks, job-description templates and onboarding checklists are directly reusable in your artefacts, and their competency model is interview currency.
- **Do this with it:** one toolkit per Phase 4 week. Steal the structure, not the wording. If an article is metered, move on — you are not signing up for anything.

#### Federal Ministry of Justice — the Labour Act itself
- **Link:** https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/laws/L1.pdf
- **Cost:** free PDF, 80 pages, no account
- **Why it matters:** primary text. Quoting section numbers correctly is the fastest way to stand out for HR Assistant/Officer roles in Nigeria.
- **Do this with it:** see Doc 4, which maps the sections you need and tells you what to memorise.

#### National Industrial Court of Nigeria
- **Link:** https://nicn.gov.ng/ (free; judgments published)
- **Why it matters:** the NICN has exclusive jurisdiction over labour and employment disputes, so its decisions — not just the Act — determine how termination and discrimination actually play out here. Reading one short case summary makes you sound like someone who has done this before.
- **Do this with it:** Week 10. Find one recent employment judgment; write 5 lines on what it means for an employer's termination process.

## 3.3 WHERE THE JOBS ARE

#### Jobberman Nigeria
- **Links:** https://www.jobberman.com/job-seeker · https://www.jobberman.com/account/cv-services · https://www.jobberman.com/learning/courses
- **Cost:** free for jobseekers (paid services exist; you don't need them)
- **What it is:** Nigeria's largest job platform, plus a free professional CV download from your profile, a free CV review, a mock-interview simulator, and published salary and hiring reports.
- **Note on their courses:** Jobberman's free catalogue contains **no HR or recruiting course** — it's soft skills, digital/gig readiness and financial literacy. Use their job-search tooling and their salary data, not their learning library.
- **Do this with it:** profile + alerts from Week 3; download the salary report for the roles you target; free CV review in Week 22.

#### MyJobMag
- **Link:** https://www.myjobmag.com/
- **Cost:** free for jobseekers (they state plainly they don't charge candidates)
- **Why it matters:** breadth. Many SMEs, NGOs and faith-based organisations post here rather than Jobberman, and it's comparatively strong for HR Assistant/Officer roles outside Lagos.
- **Do this with it:** daily alerts for `recruitment`, `talent acquisition`, `HR assistant`, `HR officer`, `people operations`.

#### LinkedIn Jobs (free tier is enough)
- **Link:** https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/ — filters: Nigeria, entry level, Human Resources / Recruiting
- **Cost:** free. You do **not** need Premium to apply; Premium is a sourcing tool you can borrow later for a single month if you ever do volume agency work.
- **Why it matters:** most formal TA roles in Lagos and Abuja appear here first, and the recruiter who posts the job is the person you then message with your portfolio link.
- **Do this with it:** saved searches with email alerts, checked twice a week, logged in your application tracker (Doc 5).

#### Other Nigerian boards worth one alert each
- **Hot Nigerian Jobs** — https://www.hotnigerianjobs.com/ · **NigerianJobs.com** — https://www.nigerianjobs.com/
- **Why:** the junior Nigerian market is fragmented; you win by covering more sources than the average applicant, not by applying to fewer.

## SUPPORT A — WHAT WAS TAKEN OUT, IN ONE PLACE

Five categories were removed on your rules, so you don't go re-finding them:

1. **Aid-gated courses** — Coursera's HR set (HRCI Talent Acquisition, Minnesota's HR Specialization, Microsoft's HR Manager certificate) and edX auditing. Free audit ended on Coursera in August 2025; what remains is a written application per course with a 150-word minimum and a wait of up to ~16 days. Saylor and OpenLearn cover the same ground with no paperwork.
2. **General-skills platforms with no HR course, HR credential or HR listings** — IBM SkillsBuild, Microsoft Learn, Google Skillshop, OpenAI Academy, Anthropic, HubSpot Academy, Trailhead, Cursa, Kaggle, OpenStax, and every generic Excel course.
3. **HR news, podcasts and newsletters** — Recruiting Brainfood, HR Brew, HR Dive, Workology, Josh Bersin, HBR's podcast, People Managing People, Chad & Cheese. Useful later; none of them teaches recruitment or hires you.
4. **Non-HR job boards, sourcing utilities, interview-practice and CV-scan tools** — We Work Remotely, Working Nomads, Remotive, Remote OK, Wellfound, The Muse; FreeSourcingTools, hireEZ, Recruiting Gym, r/recruiting; Huru, Exponent Practice, Yoodli, Big Interview, Jobscan, Class Central. Their value is now inside the plan itself: sourcing is taught directly in Doc 2 Week 14, ATS craft in Weeks 17–18, CV formatting and interview rehearsal in Doc 5.
5. **Paid, trial-only or closed** — TA Institute, Skill India, NextInHR, Great Learning's certificate, Workday/SAP/Oracle/ADP certification (no public free route exists), AIHR (its free tier ended; the Academy is paywalled), Google Interview Warmup and HubSpot's Inbound Recruiting (both retired), Talentis and the OEV HRM textbook (unreachable when verified), the ILO e-learning page, CIPD's guides-and-resources and podcasts-and-webinars pages, and SHRM's charts page (all 404).

**One judgement call you should know about:** deleting all AI and Excel training leaves a small hole, since some HR adverts ask for them. My answer: Excel is covered by artefacts you actually build (the dashboard in Weeks 17–18 teaches more than a certificate), and AI by the prompt pack and the guardrails page you write in Week 19. There is no free HR-specific AI course left to point you at — AIHR's, the last of them, is now behind its paywall. If an employer later funds a general tool course, take it then.

## SUPPORT B — HOW TO READ A RESOURCE CARD (and check it yourself)

Each card gives: **link → cost terms → time → what it is → why it matters → what to do with it.** Before you plan a week around any of them, spend 60 seconds confirming three things on the enrolment page:

1. **Is a card required to start?** If yes, it's a trial — skip it.
2. **What exactly costs money — the learning or only the certificate?** Paid certificate on free learning is acceptable; you're buying knowledge, not paper.
3. **Is there an application, essay or approval step?** Per the rule of this library, that means it's out.

If a resource fails those tests after you've started, switch to the equivalent already in this doc (Saylor ↔ Alison ↔ OpenLearn ↔ CIPD ↔ ITCILO) and keep the week's output identical. The plan survives any single course disappearing.
