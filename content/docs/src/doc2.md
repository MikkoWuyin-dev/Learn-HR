# Document 2 — The 24-Week Plan

### From zero to job-ready in HR and talent acquisition · 5–10 hours per week · ₦0

*20 October 2026 · Resources named here are documented in full in Document 3 (links, cost terms, how to use each)*

---

## HOW TO USE THIS PLAN

- **Every week is a table of 45–75 minute blocks.** Each block ends with an **output**. If you produced the output, the week passes — even if the video isn't finished.
- **Course names in bold** are resources with full cards in Doc 3 (link + cost terms there).
- **Two hard rules:** (1) never start two big courses in the same week; (2) if you fall behind, cut the reading, never cut the output.
- **Only free-to-finish resources appear here.** No applications, no financial aid, no waiting periods, no card details.
- At 5 hours a week the plan takes ~32 weeks instead of 24. Phases 4, 5 and 6 are the ones that make you employable — protect those if anything else slips.

**The spine of the plan** — three resources carry 70% of the learning:

| Resource | Why it's the backbone |
|---|---|
| **Saylor BUS301 — Human Resource Management** | Full university-style HRM course, self-paced, **free including the certificate** (pass their own exam at ≥70%) |
| **Open University OpenLearn HR courses** | Written for people with no background; a **free Statement of Participation** on completion |
| **CIPD factsheets + the three CIPD MOOCs** | The UK professional body's own framing of the job — this is the language multinational HR teams use |

### Week 14 · Sourcing — the highest-value week in the plan

| Time | Do this | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 60 min | Learn the **operators**: `AND`, `OR`, `NOT`, `"exact phrase"`, `(grouping)`, `*` wildcard. Practise each on Google until it clicks. | 10 worked examples |
| 75 min | Build **20 boolean strings** for 5 real roles. Decompose each role into *skills*, then search the skills, not the job title. | **Artefact 8** — your sourcing library |
| 60 min | Learn **X-ray searching**: `site:linkedin.com/in "role" "location" "skill"`. Build 10 searches and record how many useful hits each produced. | X-ray table |
| 60 min | Free LinkedIn search: how the limits work, keyword and filter tactics, 60-second profile triage, and a connection note that isn't cringe. | 3 outreach notes |
| 90 min | Produce a **long-list of 25 real candidates** for one role: name, current role, source, why they fit, link. This is your proof of skill. | **Artefact 9** |
| 45 min | Write the strategy paragraph you'd hand a manager: which channels, why, what you'd change after week one. | 150 words |

> **Why this week decides your interviews.** Employers can smell "I searched LinkedIn." Be the candidate who says: *I decompose the role into skills and search the skills, not the title — and if 80% of my results look wrong, I fix the query before I blame the market.*

## PHASE 6 · WEEKS 20–21 — PORTFOLIO AND PROOF

### Week 20 · Assemble the portfolio

1. Put the 15 artefacts into **one Google Doc** (or a simple site): intake template, JD good/bad pair, scorecard, sourcing library, candidate long-list, screening script, question bank, rejection templates, candidate-journey map, dashboard, data SOP, prompt library, guardrails, memo, policies.
2. Under each: **one line of context + one line on what it improves.** Nobody reads a portfolio for beauty; they read it for judgement.
3. Add a "**What I'd measure**" line to every recruiting artefact.
4. Share it ("anyone with the link") and put the link on your CV and LinkedIn.

### Week 21 · Get real proof — pick at least two

- **Volunteer to run one real requisition end to end** for an NGO, alumni association, place of worship or a founder's network: intake → sourcing → screening → shortlist. Document it, anonymised. This is the single biggest thing you can do about "no experience."
- **Mock hiring case study:** take a live Nigerian advert, run the whole process on paper, write two pages — brief, strategy, searches, long-list of 15, screen notes for 5, shortlist of 2 with scorecards, risks.
- **Two public LinkedIn posts:** one sourcing lesson, one advert you'd fix. Recruiters hire people who write clearly about hiring.
- **Five informational interviews** with Nigerian HR/TA people. Six questions each, and always: *"What would make an entry-level candidate impossible to ignore?"* Log their answers verbatim — Doc 5 uses them.

## SUPPORT B — IF YOU FALL BEHIND

| Situation | Do this |
|---|---|
| Missed a whole week | Don't double up. Resume at the next week and move the missed *outputs* into your Saturday blocks for three weeks. |
| A course is harder than expected | Switch to its gentler twin: Saylor PRDV226 ↔ BUS301; Alison short course ↔ Alison diploma. Never abandon the artefact. |
| A resource went paid | Substitute the free equivalent (Saylor ↔ Alison ↔ OpenLearn ↔ CIPD factsheet ↔ ITCILO) and keep the week's output identical. |
| Only 3 hours available that week | Keep the Saturday output block; drop course watching to 2 × 30 minutes. Outputs are the only non-negotiable. |
| You're ahead | Don't add platforms. Deepen Phase 4: more sourcing searches, a second dashboard from different data, a longer case study. |
| Motivation collapsed | Do one 25-minute session and nothing else. Also re-read your Week 3 "top 15 skills" sheet — the gap between it and your current ability is the shortest route back to wanting it. |

## SUPPORT C — WHAT "DONE" LOOKS LIKE

You are ready to apply when all five are true:

1. 15 artefacts exist and are linked from one document.
2. You can talk for 10 minutes on any HR domain and 4 minutes on Nigerian employment law, without notes.
3. You have run at least one real or fully-documented mock requisition.
4. You have a CV where every claim points to an artefact, and a LinkedIn profile worth visiting.
5. You have said your four career-changer answers out loud, five times each.

If any is false, do not start spraying applications. Fix the missing item in a week; a wasted first impression costs months.
