# Document 5 — Getting the Job

### Positioning, CV, LinkedIn, applications, interviews · Weeks 22–24 (and for the rest of your career)

*You are about to do something slightly unfair: prepare for a recruiting interview using a recruiter's own toolkit. Once you understand how hiring decisions really get made, you become a much better candidate.*

---

## 5.1 WHICH JOB YOU'RE ACTUALLY CHASING

"HR" in Nigeria is three different jobs. Know which one you're applying for, because the CV differs.

| Type | Typical titles | Who employs them | Day-to-day reality |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HR operations / generalist** | HR Assistant, HR Officer, HR Admin, Personnel Officer | Banks, FMCG, telecoms, manufacturing, NGOs, public sector | Records, attendance, leave, payroll inputs, letters, employee queries, audit support, onboarding logistics |
| **Talent acquisition** | Recruitment Officer, TA Associate/Specialist, Sourcing Specialist, Recruitment Consultant, TA Co-ordinator | Larger firms, agencies, tech, remote global clients | Reqs, adverts, sourcing, screening, scheduling, pipeline hygiene, agency/vendor liaison, reporting |
| **People ops / L&D** | People Operations Associate, L&D Officer, HR & Admin Officer | Scale-ups, NGOs, consultancies | Programmes, engagement, performance cycles, training logistics, tooling |

**Agencies hire total beginners** more readily than corporates and they train sourcing hard. A 12-month agency stint is a legitimate way to buy two years of experience in one — high pressure, KPI-driven, sometimes sales-flavored. Go in knowing it's a training ground.

**Search these titles, in this order:** Recruitment Officer · Talent Acquisition Associate · Talent Acquisition Specialist · Recruitment Consultant · Sourcing Specialist · HR Assistant · HR Officer · HR Coordinator · People Operations Associate · Recruitment Administrator · HR & Admin Officer.

Where to look (Doc 3, §3.3): Jobberman, MyJobMag, LinkedIn Jobs, Hot Nigerian Jobs, NigerianJobs. Set alerts, check twice a week, log everything.

## 5.2 YOUR POSITIONING (the pivot story)

Your position is not *"I have no experience."* It is: **"I have trained myself to run a hiring process end to end, I can show you the artefacts, and I already speak the tools."**

**The 5-line story.** Use it as your CV profile, your LinkedIn About, and your answer to "tell me about yourself." Swap in your real items:

> I'm moving into talent acquisition. Over the past six months I've trained myself through free professional curricula — Saylor's HRM course, the Open University's *Human resources: recruitment and selection*, CIPD's HR Fundamentals, and the ILO training centre's labour-administration course — and I've built the working documents of the job: intake templates, interview scorecards, a boolean and X-ray sourcing library, screening scripts, and a full recruitment funnel dashboard in Excel with time-to-fill and source-of-hire analysis. I know Nigerian employment law well enough to be useful — written particulars, notice, redundancy, sick and maternity provisions — and the data-protection rules well enough to keep candidate files clean. I'm looking for a Recruitment Officer or TA Associate role where I can own screening and sourcing from day one and grow into a generalist.

It works because it is specific, dated, verifiable, and ends with what you want. Keep it under 130 words. Never write "hard-working team player."

**Three rules:** (1) name your outputs, never your effort; (2) anchor every line to their problem — fewer unfilled reqs, faster time-to-fill, cleaner data, no compliance surprises; (3) behave like the role already: two LinkedIn posts a month on hiring topics does more than any certificate.

## 5.3 THE CV

**Structure that works:** Name · City/Country · email · phone · LinkedIn · **portfolio link** → 2-line profile → **Selected HR Projects** (4–6 bullets; this is your experience substitute) → **Skills** (grouped: Sourcing & screening / ATS & tools / HR operations / Data & reporting / Compliance) → **Certifications** (name, issuer, date, verification link) → **Education & NYSC** → **Languages · Availability · Location flexibility**.

**ATS-safety checklist — do all of these, no exceptions:**

- ☐ Single column. No tables, text boxes, multi-column layouts or headers carrying content.
- ☐ No photo for global/remote versions. (A photo is common and accepted on local Nigerian CVs — keep **two** files.)
- ☐ Standard heading names exactly: Profile · Projects · Skills · Certifications · Education.
- ☐ .docx if they ask, otherwise PDF. Filename: `CV-Firstname-Lastname-Talent-Acquisition.pdf`.
- ☐ Dates in one consistent format; job titles and company names in the body, never in a footer.
- ☐ Keywords lifted from the advert's own wording (the same 15-skill sheet you built in Doc 2, Week 3).
- ☐ No "curved arrows," icons, skill bars or rating stars — they parse as garbage.
- ☐ Manual match test: paste the advert into a plain text file, then paste your CV into another; if a required term appears 5 times in theirs and 0 in yours, fix it. (You do not need a paid scanner to learn this.)

**Rewrite every bullet as Action → Method → Result.**

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "Learned boolean search." | "Built a 25-query boolean/X-ray sourcing library across 5 roles; long-listed 15 qualified candidates in 3 days." |
| "Good communication skills." | "Ran 12 practice screening calls against a structured 6-question script; wrote candidate summaries to a shared scorecard format." |
| "Familiar with HR concepts." | "Completed Saylor BUS301 (24 h) and the Open University's *Recruitment and Selection* (12 h), then converted both into an interview kit and a 1–5 behavioural scorecard." |
| "Organised events at my association." | "Recruited and screened 14 volunteers for a 60-person programme; built the rota and retained 12 of 14 over six months." |

**Head the no-experience block `Selected HR Projects & Volunteer Work`** and list 3–5 items, each with a one-line outcome. Recruiters read that as work. Most beginners either omit it or fill it with adjectives.

**One page.** A second page is allowed only if it's the portfolio appendix.

## 5.4 LINKEDIN (free tier; no Premium)

1. **Photo** — clear face, plain background, daylight. Phone is fine. **Banner** with your target role in text.
2. **Headline = search terms:** `Aspiring Talent Acquisition Specialist | Sourcing · Screening · ATS & HR Data | Open to Recruitment Officer / TA Associate roles (Lagos · Remote)`.
3. **About** — the 5-line story.
4. **Featured** — your portfolio link and your two best artefacts (sourcing library, dashboard screenshot).
5. **Experience** — include the volunteer requisition from Week 21 as a real entry with bullets, if it happened.
6. **Certifications** — every free one you earned (Open University Statements, Saylor certificates, ITCILO credentials, AIHR). Under each, one line on what you can *do* because of it.
7. **Skills** — pin the top five from your 15-skill sheet: Sourcing, Screening, Boolean Search, ATS, Interviewing. Ask five people for a recommendation; a polite specific ask ("could you say something about how I ran the screening for your event?") gets a far better response than "please endorse."
8. **Activity** — one post every two weeks: a sourcing lesson, a before/after job ad, a fix you'd make to a careers page. Comment substantively on five recruiters' posts a week.
9. **Set Open to Work** (recruiters-only is fine) and stop treating visibility as embarrassing.
10. **Mine your own network:** search `("Talent Acquisition" OR Recruiter) AND (Lagos OR Nigeria)`, connect with 20 people: *"Hi [Name] — I'm training into TA from scratch and I've been following [company]'s hiring. I'd value 15 minutes on what your team looks for in junior recruiters; I'll bring three questions and no requests."* Notes like this get a real response rate.

## 5.5 THE APPLICATION MACHINE

**The math:** entry-level conversion runs about 1–5% per application to interview. Five interviews may take 100–150 good applications. So build a machine, not a mood: **10 tailored applications a week**, tracked, followed up on day 7 and day 14.

**Tracker columns:** Date · Company · Role · Source/link · Referrer/contact · CV version used · Cover note used · Stage · Follow-up date · Outcome · What I learned.

**The 90-word note** (email or the "message to hiring manager" box; better than a formal letter unless one is requested):

> Dear [Hiring team / name],
> Your advert for **[role]** asks for [skill 1], [skill 2] and [tool]. I've trained specifically on those: I completed a **[named course]** and built **[artefact — e.g. "a boolean sourcing library and a 1–5 interview scorecard"]**, linked below. I know the practical end of it too — [one Nigeria-specific line, e.g. "written particulars under s.7 of the Labour Act" or "referral and agency channel mix"]. I can start immediately and would value 15 minutes to walk you through the artefacts.
> [Name] · [phone] · [portfolio link]

**Portfolio link in every single application.** One Google Doc, "anyone with the link," titled `Portfolio — [Your Name], Talent Acquisition`. Test it in an incognito window before you send anything.

**When there's no advert:** a 6-line direct note to a company's HR lead, TA lead or founder (Nigerian start-ups, agencies, scaling SMEs) with one specific observation about their hiring — *"your careers page lists 9 open roles, four of them up 6+ weeks; here's a one-page take on where I'd look for those candidates"* — outperforms 40 portal applications. Do five of these a week in Week 24.

**Get on agencies' books.** Register with 3–5 Lagos/Abuja recruitment and executive-search agencies with the same one-pager. They have live reqs and short feedback loops, and observing how they treat you is free training in candidate experience.

**Nigerian application norms worth knowing:** expect requests for NYSC discharge/exemption/deployment certificates, credentials, sometimes two guarantors (letter + utility bill), and occasionally passport photographs; some SMEs still demand a Word document rather than PDF; some employers run their own portal and will not accept email. Have a single tidy folder — physical and digital — containing all of it, so you can respond within an hour. Responding fast is itself a signal.

## 5.6 INTERVIEWS: THE 15 YOU WILL ACTUALLY FACE

Practise each out loud, twice, with a timer. Write only **three bullets** per answer — bullets are recallable; scripts are brittle.

**The pivot (4)**
1. Tell me about yourself. → the 5-line story, 90 seconds.
2. Why HR, and why recruiting specifically?
3. You have no HR experience — why you?
4. Where do you want to be in three years?

**Process (5)**
5. Walk me through a hiring process from brief to day 30.
6. What makes a good job description?
7. What's a requisition, and who approves one?
8. What does "structured interview" mean and why bother?
9. Which TA metrics would you report monthly, and why those?

**Craft (3)**
10. A role has been open 45 days. What do you do first?
11. Show me how you'd search for [specific role] — say the query out loud. *(The technique is in Doc 2, Week 14: decompose into skills, search the skills not the title, then check whether the results look like the real person.)*
12. How would you prioritise eight live requisitions?

**Stakeholders and judgement (3)**
13. The hiring manager hasn't given feedback on your shortlist in nine days.
14. The manager rejects your strongest candidate for a reason you think is biased.
15. What would you do in your first 30 days here?

Then expect **two or three law questions** — answer them from Doc 4, §4.5.

**Answer architecture for every "tell me about a time":** Situation (one sentence) → what *I* did (three sentences, verbs) → Result (a number or something observable) → what I'd do differently (one sentence; this is what signals maturity). Ninety seconds total. Build a bank of **12 stories** from anywhere in your life — organising people, handling conflict, learning fast, influencing without authority, failing, using data. Transferable is fine; invented is fatal.

**The "no experience" answer, verbatim, adjusted to you:**

> *"You're right that I haven't held the title. What I have is the actual work product: here's the intake template I'd use with your hiring managers, here's the sourcing library and the 15-candidate long-list I built for a live advert, here's my funnel dashboard with time-to-fill and source of hire. I also ran a real requisition end to end for [organisation] as a volunteer. Give me one hard-to-fill req for two weeks and judge me on the pipeline."*

**Ask them four of these:** what success looks like at 90 days · live reqs per recruiter and current time-to-fill · which ATS, and who owns reporting · how TA and hiring managers work together here · the biggest hiring bottleneck right now · whether there's a path from this role into a generalist seat · what happened to the last person in the seat. Questions like these signal competence faster than any answer you can give.

**Self-score after every interview (5 minutes, while it's fresh):** ☐ I answered in under 90 seconds ☐ I gave a number at least three times ☐ I referenced their business, not just HR ☐ I showed artefacts unprompted ☐ I asked questions ☐ I said the word "process" without meaning "procedure" ☐ I closed: *"I'm very interested — what are the next steps?"* Two unscored boxes = write the fix and re-record.

**Free practice, without any tool:** record yourself on your phone answering five of the fifteen; watch it once with the sound off (posture, fidgeting), once with the sound on (fillers, pacing, whether the first sentence answers the question). Then run a live mock with a friend who reads the questions cold and is only allowed to ask "and what was the result?" Keep going until you can hear yourself smiling.

## 5.7 THE TEST TASK (very common for recruiter roles)

You may be handed a sourcing or case exercise. Standard playbook, timeboxed to 3 hours:

1. **Restate the brief** in one paragraph, plus 2–3 assumptions. This alone puts you ahead of most candidates.
2. **Decompose the role into skills**, must-have vs nice-to-have.
3. **Show your searches**: 5–8 boolean/X-ray strings, each annotated with why, plus the real result counts.
4. **Long-list 10–15** in a clean table: name · current role/company · location · fit · risk · source · link · date.
5. **Shortlist 3** with a scorecard against the agreed criteria and one-line rationale each.
6. **Flag risks and next steps**: comp misalignment, notice period, relocation, visa, reference checks required before interview.
7. **Format like work**: one PDF, your name in the footer, a 3-line executive summary at the top, no walls of text.

Submitting early and clean beats exhaustive and messy.

## 5.8 OFFER, MONEY, AND THE FIRST 90 DAYS

**"What's your expected salary?"** — a researched range plus a package reference:

> *"Based on the adverts I've seen for this grade in Lagos and my own cost research, I'm targeting ₦[X]–₦[Y] gross monthly. I'd weigh the whole package — HMO, pension, leave, training support — and I have some flexibility for the right role."*

Never name one number first if you can avoid it; never say "whatever you offer." Get your range from the Jobberman salary report and 10 live adverts, not from blogs. Ask about **probation length, notice period on your own contract, leave days, HMO, pension, hybrid policy, overtime/on-call expectations, and whether there's a training budget** (that last one is how the CIPM conversation in Doc 4 §4.4 eventually starts).

**Get it in writing before you stop applying or resign anything:** title, grade, start date, pay, benefits, probation, notice. "Verbal offer, come Monday" has burned a lot of people here.

**First 90 days, if you land it.** Days 1–15: learn the systems, the org chart, the job architecture and the top five recurring roles; sit in on everything; volunteer to take ATS notes. Days 16–45: own screening on one low-risk req; clean the pipeline; write the FAQ you wish existed. Days 46–90: produce a one-page report — funnel numbers, sources, the one problem you found, and one fix with an estimate of the saving. **That is how a junior becomes a permanent hire.**

## SUPPORT A — SCAMS, AND THE FIVE RED FLAGS

1. **Any payment request** — "processing fee," "training kit," "background check fee," "uniform/laptop deposit." Legitimate Nigerian employers do not charge candidates. Paying is how people lose ₦20,000 and get nothing.
2. **No verifiable company.** Check the CAC registration (https://search.cac.gov.ng/), the domain, the phone number, and whether a real person with a real history is hiring you.
3. **Contact from a free email address** for a "multinational," with poor formatting and urgency.
4. **Requests for BVN, NIN, bank details, or your NYSC certificate upfront** — before an offer, and for no stated lawful purpose.
5. **An offer without an interview**, or an interview that lasted three minutes and promised ₦400k for "data entry with your laptop."

Report obvious scams to the platform, warn in the relevant community, and never send originals of anything.

## SUPPORT B — REMOTE AND GLOBAL ROLES (the extra 20%)

Remote TA/coordinator roles pay in stronger currency and are the fastest way to out-earn the local junior market. Two things decide whether foreign employers hire from Nigeria — answer both before they ask:

- **Reliability:** *"I have backup power and dual internet, and I've been online for every scheduled call in the last six months."* Then prove it in month one. State your overlap hours concretely (*"I work 1pm–10pm WAT, which covers 12–1pm CET and 8–9am ET"*).
- **Payment logistics:** know how you'd be paid (contractor via a bank transfer service, or through an EOR) and be able to say *"I can invoice in USD and receive via [provider]"* — one factual sentence removes the biggest silent objection.

Also: write even tighter than usual (asynchronous cultures judge text), use "I did X, result Y" with dates, avoid idioms, and prefer companies hiring into African time zones. Search terms: `recruitment coordinator`, `talent acquisition associate`, `sourcing specialist`, `recruiter EMEA`, `people operations associate` + location filters. **Read every "remote" listing's country requirements** — "Anywhere" that quietly means "US time zone only" will eat your week.

## SUPPORT C — FOLLOW-UP TEMPLATES

**Day 0 (same-day thank-you, 4 lines):**
> Thank you for the time today — particularly the part about [specific thing they said]. It made me more confident that [role] is where I can contribute quickly: [one-line proof]. I'll send the [test task/artefact] you asked about by [day].

**Day 7 (if silent):**
> Hello [Name] — following up on my application for [Role]. I'm still very interested, and I've since added [one concrete new thing: a second dashboard, a live requisition I ran]. Happy to do a short task if that's useful at this stage.

**Day 14, final (short, warm, no guilt):**
> Closing the loop on [Role] — I assume you've moved ahead. If the requirement changes or a similar seat opens, I'd welcome the conversation; my portfolio stays here: [link]. Good luck with [their project].

**After a rejection:** one line. *"Thank you for letting me know — if there's one thing you'd have done differently in my application, I'd genuinely value hearing it."* Roughly one in five people reply with something useful, and some of them call you back for the next req.

**Never:** chase twice in one week, message their CEO on Instagram, or turn your follow-up into a grievance.
