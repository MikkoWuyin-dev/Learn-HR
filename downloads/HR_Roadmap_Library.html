READ THIS FIRST

You asked for a library of free resources that would take you from zero knowledge to job-ready in Human Resources, with a bias toward industry-standard providers. This is that library — plus the map for walking through it.

Three promises, and three honest caveats.

The promises:

  1. Everything in the main library costs ₦0 to learn from. No credit card, no trial to cancel, no "free until you want the certificate" trap — except where a card explicitly says otherwise in its Cost line. If something was "free to study, pay for the certificate," I kept it in but labelled it exactly that way, because in most cases the certificate is not what gets you hired.
  2. It is sequenced, not dumped. Roughly 80 resources are meaningless without an order. Sections 3 and 4 give you the order: seven phases, 24 weeks, each week broken into 30–60 minute steps.
  3. It is aimed at a job, not at knowledge for its own sake. You chose Talent Acquisition as your target role — the fastest realistic route into an HR paycheck — so the plan over-weights sourcing, interviewing, ATS tools and hiring metrics, while still giving you the full HR generalist grounding that makes you promotable.

The caveats you should read before spending an hour on anything:

  • A certificate is not the goal. Employability is. Almost no entry-level HR recruiter job in Lagos, Johannesburg, London or a remote global role is gated on a certificate. They are gated on: can you write a job spec, source a candidate, run a structured interview, keep an ATS clean, and talk about time-to-fill without flinching. Free certificates help you prove you did the work. The work is what helps you pass the interview.
  • "Free" changed in the last 18 months and most guides are stale. Coursera retired its free audit option on 8 August 2025 — auditing is gone, replaced by a first-module preview, and financial aid is now the only zero-cost route to a full Coursera certificate. edX still allows free audit but with a time-limited access window. FutureLearn free access gives you course content during the run and no certificate. Great Learning and Alison charge for their certificates; OpenAI Academy and Skillshop change free-tier terms frequently. Google Interview Warmup was shut down in April 2026. HubSpot retired its Inbound Recruiting course; Google Interview Warmup, HubSpot Inbound Recruiting and the Alison "Excel courses" index have all moved or closed — links here were re-checked on 20 October 2026. I have re-checked all of this (see Appendix A for what I dropped and why), but prices, free tiers and course availability move — verify on the enrolment page before you plan a week around something.
  • Nothing here replaces a credential if your target employer demands one, and Nigerian employers increasingly do. CIPM is the profession's gatekeeper in Nigeria; CIPD and SHRM are the global ones. All of them cost money. Section 6 shows you how to learn everything for ₦0 and how to build a credible case for someone else to pay for the credential later. That sequence — free knowledge first, funded credential second — is the right order, not the other way round.

How to use this document: read Section 1 (10 minutes), skim Section 2 (the beginner's orientation), then live inside Section 4 (the 24-week plan) and use Section 5 (the full library) as your reference shelf. Print or copy Appendix B's tracker. Do not start at the biggest course in the pile; that is how beginners quit in week two.

SECTION 1 — BEGINNER'S GUIDE: SET UP BEFORE YOU STUDY

1.1 The 15-word map of HR

If you remember nothing else from this section, remember this: HR exists to get business results through people — hiring them, developing them, paying them, keeping them, and letting them go well.

Everything in this plan is one of those five verbs.

1.2 The vocabulary you need on day one

Recruiters and HR people speak in shorthand. Learn these 25 now and every course you watch afterwards becomes 30% easier.

TermPlain-English meaning
Talent Acquisition (TA)Strategic, planned hiring. Differs from "recruitment" mostly in that it is data-driven and longer-term.
Recruiter / TA SpecialistThe person who runs the hiring process for open roles. Your target job.
SourcingActively hunting for candidates (often ones not looking for a job) instead of waiting for CVs to arrive.
Applicant Tracking System (ATS)The software that stores jobs, applicants, notes and pipeline stages. Greenhouse, Workday, Lever, Workable, JazzHR.
Job description (JD) / Job specThe public advert. Job analysis is the research behind it.
Requisition ("req")The formally approved request to hire for a role. No req, no hiring.
Pipeline / funnelCandidates at each stage: applied → screened → interviewed → offered → hired.
Screening callA 15–30 minute call to filter candidates before a real interview.
Structured interviewSame questions, same scoring rubric, for every candidate for the same role. The gold standard; interviewers who wing it are biased and inconsistent.
ScorecardThe written form an interviewer fills in to rate a candidate against agreed criteria.
Hiring managerThe person the new hire reports to. Your internal client.
Stakeholder managementManaging that client: chasing feedback, aligning on the spec, pushing back on unrealistic demands.
Time-to-fill / time-to-hireDays from req opening to offer accepted (or from application to offer). Core TA metric.
Cost-per-hireTotal hiring cost ÷ hires.
Offer acceptance rateOffers accepted ÷ offers made.
Quality of hireHow well new hires perform/retention at 6–12 months. The metric everyone claims to use and few actually measure.
Source of hireWhich channel (LinkedIn, referral, job board) produced the people you actually hired. Tells you where to spend.
Headcount & workforce planningDeciding how many people, with which skills, before hiring starts.
OnboardingThe first 30/60/90 days. Bad onboarding is the #1 cause of early attrition.
Employee lifecycleAttract → hire → onboard → develop → reward → retain → separate. The spine of HR.
HR generalist / HRBPA person who covers the whole lifecycle (generalist) or partners with a business unit (HR Business Partner).
HRISThe system of record for employee data (as distinct from the ATS, which is for applicants).
Total rewardsEverything of value an employee gets: base pay, bonuses, benefits, well-being, career, recognition. WorldatWork's five pillars.
Employee relations (ER)The handling of conflict, discipline, grievances and investigations.
RedundancyLosing a job because the role has gone, not because you performed badly. In Nigeria the Labour Act's "last in, first out" rule (s.20) applies to covered workers.

Action: write these on one page of your own notes, in your own words, and keep it open while you study. By week 6 you will be able to add a definition column of your own.

1.3 How to actually learn this (the method, not the motivation)

Most people who self-teach HR fail in the same way: they watch 40 hours of video, feel productive, and cannot answer a single interview question. Fix it with four rules.

  • Rule 1 — Never watch without producing. For every 60 minutes of video or reading, spend 20 minutes producing something: a one-page summary, a written definition, a template. If you cannot produce it, you did not learn it.
  • Rule 2 — Active recall beats re-watching. At the start of each session, close everything and write down, from memory, what you learned last session. Five minutes. This single habit roughly halves forgetting.
  • Rule 3 — Space it. 45 minutes a day for 5 days beats 4 hours on Sunday. Your plan is deliberately built in small chunks for this reason.
  • Rule 4 — Build in public. Your portfolio (Phase 6) is what converts "I did some free courses" into "she can actually hire people." Start collecting artefacts in week 1, not week 21.

1.4 Your free toolkit (set up once, 45 minutes)

  1. One email address and one password manager entry dedicated to your HR job search. You will sign up for 15+ platforms; keep it clean.
  2. Notes: Google Docs or Microsoft Word Online — both are free in a browser and both give you a shareable link you can paste into job applications and your LinkedIn. Create one document called `HR Learning Log`.
  3. Spreadsheet: Google Sheets or Excel Online (free in the browser). Create `Recruiting Tracker.xlsx` — you will use it in Phase 5 for a real hiring funnel.
  4. A dedicated folder in Drive/OneDrive called `HR Portfolio`. Everything you produce from week 1 goes here.
  5. LinkedIn account, free tier. Free is enough. Optimise in Phase 7, not before — you want something worth showing.
  6. Free AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, free tiers). Use it as a tutor and sparring partner — "quiz me on Nigerian notice periods" — not as a ghostwriter. Full guidance in Phase 6.
  7. A calendar block. Book it now: e.g. Tue 8–9pm, Wed 8–9pm, Fri 7–8pm, Sat 9–12, Sun 1 hr review. The plan is 5–10 hrs/week; if it isn't in the calendar it will not happen.

1.5 The 3-test "is this genuinely free?" checklist

Apply this to every resource you ever find, including ones in this library:

  1. Can I finish the whole thing without entering card details? If the site asks for payment before showing the module list, it is a trial, not a free course.
  2. What exactly am I paying for? Separate the learning from the certificate. A paid certificate on free learning is usually still a good deal for you as a beginner — the knowledge transfers even if the PDF doesn't.
  3. Is the credential verifiable? A certificate with no verification URL or credential ID is a participation ribbon. Prefer resources whose output you can see (a project, a portfolio page, a badge with a public link) over ones whose output is a JPG of a diploma.

1.6 The three "free certificate" levers most beginners don't know about

  • Coursera Financial Aid. Since August 2025 the old free audit is gone; financial aid is the zero-cost route. You apply per individual course (not per Specialization), answer two prompts with a 150-word minimum, and wait up to ~16 days. Coursera now describes it as a discount whose size depends on your application and country — many learners get full access and the certificate for $0, some get 75–90%. You get 180 days to finish each approved course. Strategy: submit aid applications for all courses in a specialization on day 1, then keep studying in the free Preview while you wait, and never start a free trial while an application is pending (it cancels the application).
  • LinkedIn Learning free courses and library access. LinkedIn Learning is normally paid via Premium Career, but (a) there is a rotating set of genuinely free courses and paths, and (b) Class Central maintains a vetted list of hundreds of hours of free LinkedIn Learning courses that still include a free certificate. Search that list before you consider paying anything.
  • Employer or government-funded access. IBM SkillsBuild, Microsoft Learn, HubSpot Academy and Salesforce Trailhead are free to everyone; SkillsBuild credentials are issued on Credly at no cost. Note for Nigeria: Microsoft's Virtual Training Days used to hand out free exam vouchers, but voucher eligibility has excluded Nigeria — plan on free training, and treat any exam as a funded-later item.

1.7 What "job-ready" means for a first HR/TA role (so you know the target)

By the end of this plan you should be able to, out loud, in an interview, with examples:

  • take a hiring brief from a manager and turn it into a job analysis + job description + scorecard;
  • source candidates on LinkedIn and Google using Boolean and X-ray search, and explain your search strategy;
  • run a 15-minute screening call and write a summary that a hiring manager can act on;
  • explain structured interviewing, why it beats "gut feel," and how you'd reduce bias in a panel;
  • keep an ATS clean: stages, notes, tags, dispositions with reasons, and no expired candidate data lying around;
  • build a recruitment dashboard in Excel/Sheets showing funnel conversion, time-to-fill and source of hire — and say what you'd change about the process based on it;
  • state the basics of Nigerian employment law a recruiter will actually be asked about: written particulars of employment, notice periods, redundancy (LIFO), sick leave, maternity, working hours, the national minimum wage, and pensions;
  • talk about how you use AI in hiring and where you draw the ethical line;
  • say what a good candidate experience looks like, including how you reject people.

That is the bar. Everything in Section 4 exists to get you over it.

Nigerian Labour Act, Cap L1, Laws of the Federation of Nigeria (full text PDF)

  • Link: https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/laws/L1.pdf
  • Cost: free
  • What it is: the statute itself, as published by the Federal Ministry of Justice. 80 pages, arranged by part.
  • Key provisions to be able to quote (I read the arrangement of sections in this PDF): s.7 written particulars of terms of employment · s.8 medical examination (restricted) · s.9 contracts generally · s.11 termination by notice · s.13 hours of work and overtime · s.14 provision of transport · s.15 periodicity of wage payment · s.16 sick leave · s.17 duty to provide work · s.18 annual holidays with pay · s.19 calculation of leave pay and sickness benefits · s.20 redundancy · Part II (ss.23–33) regulates recruiting, including licensing of recruiters — relevant to you, and rarely mentioned by candidates.
  • Why you need it: quoting section numbers, confidently and correctly, is the fastest way to stand out for an HR Assistant/Officer role in Nigeria.
  • Do this with it: Ctrl-F each section in week 10; write a one-line plain-English summary of each into your glossary. And always check whether an amendment or a sector law supersedes it — this Act's general application excludes administrative/executive/technical/professional employees, which is why contracts and policy matter so much in practice.

National minimum wage — the figure you must get right

  • Verified position (Oct 2026): the National Minimum Wage (Amendment) Act 2024 (signed 29 July 2024) raised the statutory floor from ₦30,000 to ₦70,000 per month; the Act sets a three-year review cycle, so the next review is due 1 June 2027. As of October 2026 no new national figure has been agreed (the NLC has been pressing for a committee; labour proposals have ranged widely and are proposals only). Individual states have set higher rates — but those bind their own civil servants (e.g. Lagos ₦85,000, Imo ₦104,000, Rivers ₦85,000), not private employers, who owe the national floor.
  • Why you need it: salary questions come up in every first interview, and misquoting the floor is an instant credibility loss in an HR interview.
  • Do this with it: state it the way it's stated above — dated, sourced, hedged — and verify once more the week you interview.

SECTION 5 — BONUS: GETTING THE JOB (POSITIONING, APPLICATIONS, INTERVIEWS)

You are going to do something slightly unfair here: you will prepare for a recruiting interview using a recruiter's own toolkit. Once you understand how hiring decisions really get made, you become a much better candidate — and you can say so.

5.1 First, the job you're actually chasing

"HR" in Nigeria, in practice, is three different jobs. Know which one you're applying for:

TypeTypical titlesWho employs themDay-to-day reality
HR operations / generalistHR Assistant, HR Officer, HR Admin, Personnel OfficerBanks, FMCG, telecoms, manufacturing, NGOs, civil serviceFiles, records, attendance, leave, payroll inputs, letters, employee queries, audit support, onboarding logistics
Talent acquisitionRecruitment Officer, TA Associate, Sourcing Specialist, Recruiter, Talent Acquisition ExecutiveLarger firms, agencies, tech/startups, remote global clientsReqs, adverts, sourcing, screening calls, scheduling, pipeline hygiene, vendor/agency liaison, reporting
People ops / HRBP-trackPeople Operations Associate, HR Business Partner (not entry-level), L&D OfficerTech, scale-ups, multinationals, consultanciesProgrammes, engagement, learning, performance cycles, data and tooling

Agencies (e.g. executive search and recruitment firms in Lagos/Abuja) hire total beginners more readily than corporates do — and they train sourcing hard. A 12-month agency stint is a very legitimate way to buy two years of experience in one. The trade-off: high pressure, KPI/pressured sales-like targets, sometimes "you must live near Ikeja" — go in knowing it's a training ground, not a retirement plan.

Titles to search (put each into your alerts, in this order of value for you): `Recruitment Officer`, `Talent Acquisition Associate`, `Talent Acquisition Specialist`, `Recruitment Consultant`, `Sourcing Specialist`, `HR Assistant`, `HR Officer`, `HR Coordinator`, `People Operations Associate`, `Recruitment Administrator`, `TA Coordinator`, `Junior Recruiter`, `HR & Admin Officer`, `Staffing Coordinator`.

5.2 The Nigerian market, honestly

  • Volume: most Nigerian HR vacancies are "HR Officer/Assistant" hybrids that include admin and payroll support. Pure TA roles are concentrated in Lagos (Ikeja, Victoria Island, Lekki, Ikoyi), Abuja, and a growing set of remote-first tech companies.
  • What's asked: 1–2 years' experience appears in most adverts even for junior roles — it is a wish list, not a law. Nigerian HR job adverts frequently list CIPM (or "CIPM-part-qualified") as an advantage; several list it as required for officer-grade and above.
  • Documents: expect to be asked for CV (often Word), NYSC discharge/exemption certificate, credentials, a guarantor or two (sometimes with a letter and utility bill), and sometimes passport photographs. Preparing these in a single tidy folder now removes friction later.
  • Salary reality: junior HR/recruitment in Nigeria has typically clustered in the ₦100k–₦350k/month range for many employers, with Lagos tech, banking, oil & gas and multinationals paying notably more — but published data is thin, inflation moves fast, and remote roles for foreign companies are a different economy entirely. Get your number from Jobberman's salary report and from live adverts, not from blogs.
  • Red flags — you must know these: any "job" that asks you to pay for training, a "recruitment kit," a background check, or a "processing fee" is a scam. Legit employers do not charge candidates. Verify the company on the CAC register (https://search.cac.gov.ng/), check the domain matches the real company, and never send bank details, BVN, or your NIN to an unverified "recruiter." Report obvious scams to the platform.

5.3 Positioning: how to tell your story as a career-changer

Your position is not "I have no experience." Your position is: "I have trained myself to run a hiring process end to end, I can show you the artefacts, and I already speak the tools."

The 5-line story (About section, and your answer to "tell me about yourself"):

I'm moving into talent acquisition. Over the past six months I've trained myself through free professional curricula — Saylor's HRM course, the Open University's recruitment and selection course, CIPD's HR Fundamentals, and ILO/ITCILo labour-administration training — and I've built the working documents of the job: intake templates, scorecards, a boolean/X-ray sourcing library, screening scripts, and a full recruitment funnel dashboard in Excel with time-to-fill and source-of-hire analysis. I know Nigerian employment law well enough to be useful (Labour Act ss.7–20, redundancy, notice, sick and maternity provisions) and NDPA/GDPR well enough to keep candidate data safe. I'm looking for a Recruitment Officer or TA Associate role where I can own screening and sourcing from day one and grow into a generalist.

That paragraph works because it is specific, dated, verifiable, and ends with what you want. Fill in your real details, keep it to 130 words, and never say "hard-working team player."

Three positioning rules:

  1. Name your outputs, not your effort. "Completed 80 hours of structured study" is nothing. "Wrote a candidate-retention policy and a 25-query sourcing library" is a person who can do the job.
  2. Anchor to their problem. Every application line should imply: fewer unfilled reqs, faster time-to-fill, cleaner pipeline data, no compliance surprises.
  3. Be the person who already behaves like one. Two LinkedIn posts a month on hiring topics, plus polite engagement with other recruiters' posts, do more than any certificate.

5.4 The CV (ATS-safe, one page, Nigerian-appropriate)

Structure that works: Name + City/Country + email + phone + LinkedIn + portfolio link → 2-line profile → Selected HR Projects (4–6 bullets, this is your experience substitute) → Skills (grouped: Sourcing & screening / ATS & tools / HR operations / Data & reporting / Compliance) → Certifications (name, issuer, date, link) → Education & NYSC → Languages & availability.

Format rules for parsing: single column; no tables/text boxes/columns in headers; no photo on CVs for global/remote roles (common and accepted in Nigeria for local ones — keep two versions); .docx if they ask, otherwise PDF; standard headings named exactly "Work Experience"/"Projects"/"Education"; consistent date format; no headers/footers for anything important.

Do/Don't bullet rewrites (memorise the pattern, use your own facts):

WeakStrong (Action → Method → Result)
"Learned boolean search.""Built a 25-query boolean/X-ray sourcing library for 5 hard-to-fill roles, producing 15 qualified candidates in 3 days."
"Good communication skills.""Ran 12 practice screening calls using a structured 6-question script; documented candidate summaries to a shared scorecard format."
"Familiar with HR concepts.""Completed Saylor BUS301 (HRM, 24 h) and Open University's Recruitment and Selection (12 h), and translated both into a reusable interview kit and 1–5 behavioural scorecard."
"Organised events at church/association.""Coordinated a 60-person volunteer programme: recruited and screened 14 helpers, built a rota, and retained 12 of 14 over 6 months."

The "no experience" section — call it what it is. Head it `Selected HR Projects & Volunteer Work`, list 3–5 items, each with a one-line outcome. Recruiters read this as legitimate work; almost every beginner either omits it or fills it with adjectives.

5.5 LinkedIn, without paying anyone

  1. Photo (clear face, plain background, natural light — your phone is enough), banner with your target role text.
  2. Headline: `Aspiring Talent Acquisition Specialist | Sourcing · Screening · ATS & HR Data | Open to Recruitment Officer / TA Associate roles (Lagos · Remote)`. Keywords are what search finds you for; "seeking opportunities" alone is invisible.
  3. About: the 5-line story above.
  4. Featured: your portfolio link + the two artefacts you're proudest of (sourcing library, dashboard screenshot).
  5. Experience: include the volunteer/mock requisition as a real entry ("Talent Acquisition — Volunteer, [Org], Mar–May 2026") with bullets. This is honest if it actually happened; do it in Week 22 so it does.
  6. Certifications: add every free credential (Open University SoPs, IBM Credly badges, OpenAI Academy, HubSpot, ITCILO). Under each, one line on what you can do because of it.
  7. Skills: pin the top 5 from your Week 3 spreadsheet (Sourcing, Screening, Boolean Search, ATS, Interviewing). Ask 5 people to endorse/vouch — a kind ask gets a ~60% response rate.
  8. Activity: 1 post every two weeks: a lesson learned, an ad you'd fix, a before/after job description. Set creator mode on; comment substantively on 5 recruiter posts weekly.
  9. Never set "Open to Work" visible only to recruiters and then apply as if nobody can see it — it's fine, use it; and never post "please refer me" without your artefacts attached.

Sourcing your own network (do this once, 45 min): search LinkedIn for `("Talent Acquisition" OR Recruiter) AND (Lagos OR Nigeria)`, connect with 20 people using: "Hi [Name] — I'm training into TA from scratch and I've been following [their company]'s hiring. I'd value 15 minutes on what your team looks for in junior recruiters; I'll bring three questions and no requests." Response rates on that kind of note are surprisingly good.

5.6 Applications that get answered

The volume math: entry-level Nigerian/remote applications convert at roughly 1–5% to interview. To land 5 interviews you may need 100–150 good applications. So build a machine, not a mood: 10 tailored applications a week in Weeks 23–24, tracked in your sheet, with follow-ups on day 7 and day 14.

Cover note (the 90-word email/field note — better than a formal letter unless they ask for one):

Dear [Name or "Hiring team"], Your advert for [Role] asks for [skill 1], [skill 2] and [tool]. I've trained specifically for those: I completed [course X] and built [artefact Y — e.g. "a boolean sourcing library and a 1–5 interview scorecard"], which I've attached/linked. I know the practical end of it too — [1 sentence about Nigerian context: "written particulars under s.7 of the Labour Act," or "referral and agency channel mix"]. I can start immediately and would like 15 minutes to show you the artefacts. [Your name] · [phone] · [portfolio link]

Portfolio link in every application. One Google Doc, shared "anyone with the link," titled `Portfolio — [Your Name], Talent Acquisition`.

When there's no advert (the best-kept secret in job hunting): a 6-line cold note to a company's HR/TA lead or founder (Nigerian start-ups, agencies, SMEs scaling teams) with one specific observation about their hiring ("your careers page lists 9 open roles, and 4 have been up 6+ weeks — here's a 2-page take on where I'd look for those candidates") outperforms 40 portal applications. Do 5 of these a week in Week 24.

Recruiter-agency route: register with 3–5 Lagos/Abuja recruitment agencies and send them the same one-pager. Agencies have live reqs and short feedback loops; getting "on their books" as a junior TA candidate can produce an interview in weeks, not months. (And observe how they treat you — it's free training.)

5.7 Interviews: the 40 questions you will actually face

Practice each out loud, twice, with a timer. Write the answer in 3 bullet points only — bullets are recallable, scripts are memorable, and memorised answers fail the first interruption.

Motivation & story (5) 1. Tell me about yourself. 2. Why HR? 3. Why recruiting, specifically? 4. You have no HR experience — why you? 5. Where do you want to be in three years?

Role knowledge (7) 6. Walk me through a hiring process from brief to day 30. 7. What's the difference between recruitment and talent acquisition? 8. What makes a good job description? 9. What's a requisition, and who approves it? 10. What does "structured interview" mean and why bother? 11. What's candidate experience, and where does it usually break? 12. Which TA metrics would you report monthly, and why those?

Sourcing & screening (8) 13. A role has been open 45 days. What do you do first? 14. Show me how you'd search for a [specific role] — say the query out loud. 15. What is X-ray searching? 16. A candidate has no idea they're looking. How do you approach them? 17. What do you listen for in a 15-minute screen? 18. How do you tell a genuinely strong CV from a well-written one? 19. How would you source in Nigeria for a remote US-hours role? 20. Which channels would you prioritise for blue-collar vs professional hires here?

Stakeholder management (6) 21. The hiring manager hasn't given feedback on your shortlist for 9 days. 22. The manager wants "5 years' experience with a tool that's 3 years old." 23. The manager rejects your best candidate for a reason you think is biased. 24. Two managers want the same single headcount. 25. How do you say "we will not fill this at this salary" professionally? 26. Tell me about a time you influenced someone who didn't report to you.

Compliance & Nigeria (6) 27. What must a Nigerian employment contract/letter contain? 28. Explain notice periods under the Labour Act. 29. What is redundancy under s.20, and what does LIFO mean in practice? 30. Maternity and sick leave entitlements under the Act? 31. AI & modern practice (3) 38. How do you use AI in hiring, and what would you never use it for? 39. What are the risks of AI screening? 40. What's changing in TA that our team should care about? Answer architecture for behavioural questions (use it for every "tell me about a time"): Situation (1 sentence) → What I did (3 sentences, verbs) → Result (number or observable outcome) → What I'd do differently (1 sentence, shows maturity). 90 seconds total.

"No experience" — the answer that works: "You're right that I haven't held the title. What I have done is build the actual work products: here's the intake template I'd use with your hiring managers, here's the sourcing library and the 15-candidate long-list I built for [role], here's my funnel dashboard. I've also run [X] as a volunteer hire for [org]. Give me one hard-to-fill req for two weeks and you'll have a pipeline to judge me on." Then mean it — that's why Weeks 21–22 exist.

Ask them (pick 4): success at 90 days · number of live reqs per recruiter and current time-to-fill · which ATS and who owns reporting · how the TA and hiring-manager relationship works here · what the biggest hiring bottleneck is right now · is there a path to HR generalist/HRBP · what happened to the last person in this seat · what the first 30 days look like.

5.8 The test task (very common for recruiter roles)

You may be given a sourcing or case exercise. Standard playbook:

  1. Restate the brief in one paragraph and list 2–3 assumptions — this alone puts you ahead of most candidates.
  2. Decompose the role into skills, not job titles; state must-have vs nice-to-have.
  3. Show your searches: 5–8 boolean/X-ray strings, each annotated with why, plus the results you actually got (counts, sample profiles, links).
  4. Long-list 10–15 in a clean table: name, current title/company, location, why a fit, potential risk, source, link, date.
  5. Shortlist 3 with a scorecard against the criteria and one-line rationale each.
  6. Flag risk and next steps: comp misalignment, notice period, relocation, visa, "would need a reference check before interview."
  7. Format the deliverable like work: one PDF or one tidy sheet, your name in the footer, no walls of text, a 3-line executive summary at the top.

Timebox yourself to 3 hours when practising. Submitting early and clean beats submitting exhaustive and messy.

5.9 Offer, negotiation, and the first 90 days

  • "What's your expected salary?" — answer with a range anchored on research, and give it in the same breath as flexibility on structure: "Based on the adverts I've seen for this grade in Lagos and my own cost research, I'm targeting ₦[X]–₦[Y] gross monthly. I'd be more interested in the whole package — HMO, pension, training support, leave — and I have some flexibility for the right role." Never name a single number first if you can avoid it; never say "whatever you offer."
  • Ask about: gross vs net (Nigeria: PAYE, pension 8% employee share, housing/ITF deductions where applicable — ask how they structure it), HMO coverage, leave days, probation length, notice period on your contract, hybrid policy, training budget and CIPM support (ask — some employers do fund it), overtime/on-call expectations for an HR role, and who you report to.
  • Get it in writing before you resign anything or stop applying: offer letter with title, start date, grade, pay, benefits, probation, notice. In Nigeria, "verbal offer, come Monday" has burned a lot of people.
  • First 90 days if you land it: Days 1–15, learn the systems, the org chart, the job architecture and the top 5 recurring roles; sit in on everything; ask to take ATS notes. Days 16–45, own screening for one low-risk req; clean the pipeline; write the FAQ you wished existed. Days 46–90, produce a one-page report: funnel numbers, sources, one problem you found, one fix you propose with an estimate of the saving. This is how a junior becomes a permanent hire — and how you get the CIPM conversation.
  • Remote/global additions: keep a plain "how I work async" line in your CV (your overlap hours in WAT/GMT, tools, written-first style); write your applications in tighter, more direct English; prefer asynchronous-friendly companies; and be ready for the two questions every foreign employer asks an African applicant — payment logistics (have a Wise/Payoneer/bank plan ready and factual) and reliability (power/internet backup; state your setup briefly and confidently in the interview, then prove it in the first month).

APPENDIX C — BEGINNER FAQ

Do I need a degree? For many Nigerian HR Officer roles employers ask for one (usually any discipline; HR/Management/Psychology/Law help), and public-sector roles almost always require it. In private SMEs, agencies, tech companies and remote global roles, a demonstrable portfolio plus relevant certificates can carry you into a junior/admin seat. If you lack a degree, target agencies, start-ups, NGOs and remote-first companies first — and plan a part-time degree later if you want corporate banking/FMCG.

Is 24 weeks realistic, honestly? For "capable and employable at junior level," yes — that's roughly 175 focused hours plus artefacts. For "confidently handling 8 live requisitions alone," expect 6–12 months on the job. Anyone promising otherwise is selling something.

Should I pay for the Alison certificate? No. Pay for things that are verifiable and exclusive (a university statement, a Credly badge, an association credential). Alison's knowledge is free; its PDF is a marketing product for them. Your budget is better saved for CIPM or CIPD later.

What if I can only do 5 hours a week? Cut Phase 2's breadth, not Phase 4. Weeks 13–17 are what make you employable. Stretch everything else.

What if a course I planned is now paid? Everything here was verified on 20 October 2026, but platforms change terms. Check the enrolment page; if it flipped to paid, swap in the free equivalent (Alison ↔ Saylor ↔ OpenLearn ↔ CIPD factsheet ↔ ITCILO). The plan is designed to survive any single course disappearing.

Can I do this from a phone? Mostly, yes: Saylor, Alison, OpenLearn, Coursera, LinkedIn, HubSpot, OpenAI Academy and ITCILO are all usable on mobile. The two things that need a proper keyboard are the Excel/Sheets dashboard and writing your artefacts — borrow a laptop at a cyber café or library for those, or use the free mobile spreadsheet apps which are adequate for pivots on small data.

Where does the "Udemy" part of my brief go? Nowhere, by your own rule: the HR and recruiting courses worth taking on Udemy are paid, and the "free Udemy" claims circulating online were wrong on inspection. If you later find a genuinely free, comprehensive HR course there, run it through the three tests in §1.5 and add it.

What should I do in week 1 if I only have 3 hours? OpenLearn Developing Your Skills as an HR Professional (first half), then write the 10-line "what HR is for" summary and update your glossary. That's a real week.
